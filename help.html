What the app does

The Tacheo phone app carries your caves from CartoTable (the cave survey and map side of Tacheo) on your phone. It:

  • shows the line plot in plan, profile and 3-D, over map imagery and the ground's shape, with no signal needed once a cave is on the phone;
  • finds where you are by station, the quickest way out, and how far it is between two stations;
  • lists leads nearest first, with their notes, and the trip books and trip reports;
  • plans a trip by travel time;
  • sends photos of survey book pages and sketches to your computer, where Tacheo reads the data pages into survey data;
  • connects to Tacheo on your computer, over your Wi-Fi or from anywhere through Tailscale;
  • surveys and sketches: shots straight from a Tacheo or SAP6 over Bluetooth (Android app), legs and splays sorted as they come in, the plan and extended profile drawn as you go, and sketched over in NSS symbols.
Updating a unit's firmware, calibrating it and its diagnostics still need Tacheo on a computer. The phone takes shots from the unit, reads its battery, calibration and trip, keeps its station names in step and fetches its cross sections.

Install it

Android

  1. Open the downloads page in Chrome and tap Download for Android.
  2. Open the download and tap Install. The first time, Android asks to let your browser install apps: allow it.
  3. If Play Protect says it doesn't know the developer: More details → Install anyway.

Opened the link inside Messenger or another message app? Use that app's ⋮ menu → Open in Chrome first. If you installed the web copy from Chrome earlier, it still works; the app from the download adds the link to your computer.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open the downloads page in Safari and tap Download for iPhone / iPad, then Allow.
  2. Open Settings → Profile Downloaded → Install. Tacheo appears on the home screen.

Or in Safari: Share → Add to Home Screen.

Survey and sketch

A survey here is one trip. It keeps the shots, the plan and profile sketches and the cross sections together on the phone, works with no signal, and goes to Tacheo on your computer at the end.

Start a trip

Home → Survey. One page: the Tacheo units and their controls at the top, CrossTalk, then the survey the shots go into, shown like a page of the survey book - 13 stations a page (tap 13 stations a page to change it), each with the leg that reached it (front sight, backsight under it) and its LRUD. ◀ ▶ at the bottom turn the pages; the newest page shows when a shot lands. Pick another survey, or ＋ New survey…, from the list at the top of it; Plan, Profile, Trip and All shots open the survey for sketching and the rest. A new survey asks three things. The phone remembers your answers for the next trip.

  1. The cave. Its name, whether it's a known cave or a new cave, and where the survey starts: at an entrance (say which one) or at a station already surveyed (say which - B12, say - and the survey ties in there). County, state, and the landowner or access note if you want them; they go to your computer with the trip.
  2. The survey. The trip's name, the station prefix (A for A0, A1 …) and the first station's number - 0 at an entrance, so the entrance is A0 - the date, the units you want to see, the team and the instrument. Shots per leg: 3 (the default) means three shots in a row that agree are one leg, as TopoDroid and the Tacheo's FSx3 do; pick 1 if every shot is a leg. Declination, east positive, if you know it (it can be changed later; the shots are kept as read).
  3. GPS at the entrance station (only when the survey starts at an entrance). Stand at the station - or as close as you can with open sky - and tap 📍 Take the GPS point at A0. Hold the phone still: it averages the readings and keeps the point by itself when it's steady (usually 10 to 45 seconds), and names it GPS A0. No signal at the entrance? Skip, and take it later from the Data page or the Trip page.

The GPS point goes into the survey files as the fix for that station (#Fix in the .srv, *fix in the .svx, fix in the .th), so the survey lands in the right place in CartoTable. Where the cave on the phone has an elevation model, the fix uses the ground's height from it; otherwise the phone's GPS height, which is rough - check it.

Take shots

With a Tacheo or SAP6 (Android app): tap the 📡 chip at the top, pick the unit from the list (on the unit: Bluetooth → Phone On), and shoot as normal. Each shot comes in as you take it:

  • Three shots that agree (within 5 cm or 1 %, and 1.5°, both changeable on the Trip page) become one leg from the station you're at to the next new one. The phone buzzes twice for a leg, once for a splay.
  • Anything else is a splay from the station you're at - shoot the walls, floor and ceiling as much as you like; they're the guides for the sketch.
  • A backsight - three agreeing shots back along the leg just made, or shots the Tacheo labels as backsights - is kept with that leg (it shows as backsight). It doesn't move you on.
  • The Tacheo's own labels are kept with each shot: FSx3, BS, BS check, and its LRUD shots with their letter (an amber check tag is one the unit marked Check).

Typing them (any phone, and for a Suunto-and-tape trip): ＋ Add a shot. From and To are filled in for you; leave To empty for a splay. L, R, U/D (as 3/1.5) are added as LRUD shots at the From station.

The station you're at is shown at the top of the Data page. Change it to go back to a station already surveyed (a side passage, the other side of a loop) or to start a new prefix.

Fix a shot

Tap any row on the Data page. A leg shows each of its shots with the time. You can rename From and To, set which way the leg goes in the extended profile (→ Right, ← Left, ↕ Vertical, Ignore), mark it Surface, Duplicate or Commented out, turn a leg's shots back into splays, give a splay a To station to make it a leg, or delete it.

Sketch

  1. Plan or Profile at the top. The legs (red), stations, splays (grey; LRUDs amber; splays marked to wall dark green) and a grid in your units are drawn for you, north up in the plan. The active station - where new shots go from - is the green dot with a ring.
  2. Pick a tool along the bottom. Tap it again to open its palette:
    ✋ MoveOne finger moves the drawing; two fingers move and zoom with any tool. Tap a station for its menu; tap a cross section for its menu, or drag it somewhere else.
    〰 LinesWall, lower-level wall, unsurveyed wall, floor step / ledge, ceiling step, pit / drop, dome edge, drip line, water edge, stream (draw it downstream), rock / boulder, slope, rough passage outline. A wall is turned round by itself so the passage is on its left (the way Therion reads walls).
    ▦ AreasWater / pool, sump, sand, gravel, mud / clay, breakdown, bedrock pillar, flowstone, guano, ice. Draw round the area. An area drawn over another of the same kind joins it.
    ✳ SymbolsStalactites, stalagmites, column, soda straws, helictites, rimstone, cave pearls, popcorn, a water drip, a small pool, pit depth, dome height, ceiling height, climb, squeeze, lead (?), entrance, dig, hazard, bats, bones, boulder, breakdown block, and text. Air flow, water flow and slope arrows: touch and drag the way they point. Symbols are sized by the zoom you place them at: zoomed in, they're small.
    ✎ PenFreehand in eight colours and three widths, for notes and anything the symbols don't cover.
    ⬚ SelectTap a line, area or symbol to pick it: drag it to move it, drag one of its points to reshape it. Hold it (or tap it again) for its menu: change its type or colour, flip it (a wall's yellow tick points into the passage), make a symbol bigger or smaller, edit its text, delete it. Holding a line with the line or area tool opens the same menu.
    ⌫ Rub outTap a line, area or symbol to remove it. Rub across the sketch to cut: lines are cut where you rub, and areas lose what's under it - rub a hole in one, or right across to split it in two.
    ✚ Cross sectionTap a station for its cross section (below).
    ↶ ↷ ⤢ ⌖Undo and redo (the plan, the profile and each section separately), fit everything on the screen, centre on the active station.
    ⚙ Quick settingsSplays, station names and grid on or off; snap lines to the ends of lines (a line started or ended near another's end joins it); clip lines that overshoot a wall (a floor step drawn past a wall is cut back to it); bring a new station back to the middle; fade the sketch away from the active station; symbols sized by zoom. Also the phone's compass, a section at every station, and undo the last reading (a leg it completed goes back to splays and you're back at the station before).
  3. Draw as the shots come in - the sketch and the shots are on the same page, so a splay to a wall is the guide for the wall line.
Each thing you draw is tied to the station nearest it. If a shot is fixed later and the station moves, the sketch near it moves with it.

A station's menu

With ✋, tap a station: make it the active station (new shots go from there - the Tacheo is told too), centre here, cross section here, receive a cross section from the Tacheo, and its splays, each with two switches: to wall (used for the station's cross section) and off (left out of the sketch, the sections and the exports).

Cross sections

Tap ✚ then a station. The section is made from that station's splays in the cut (the ones marked to wall, when there are three or more), or from its LRUDs, and drawn the way a finished map draws one:

  • On the plan, the cut is square to the passage (the bisector of the legs in and out). The cut line is left out across the passage, a little wider than the section; one end has a corner mark pointing the way the section looks (up the survey), the other carries on as the leader, a short stub then straight across, down or at 45° to the section.
  • The section has its walls in heavy line, the floor (anything facing down) in light line, a block of limestone bricks round it, a height bar on the right and the station's name above. Sections go alternately either side of the survey.
  • Tap one for its menu: open it to draw its walls, look the other way, leader to the other side, put it back beside its station, delete it. Drag it with ✋ to move it; the leader stays straight, square or at 45°.
  • Its editor shows the splays in the cut (solid) and out of it (dashed), with the left wall on the left. Draw its walls with the line tool; the drawn walls then replace the outline from the splays, and the finished section in the corner updates as you go.
  • In the profile, a section is cut upright through the station; at a station in a shaft (steep legs both ways) it's cut level and looked at from above.
  • Sections taken on a Tacheo (Tools → Cross section) come in the same style from the unit's ✚ Sections.
  • Receive one straight from the Tacheo: with ✋, tap the station, then 📡 Receive a cross section from the Tacheo. Drag a line across the passage there and lift your finger on the side the section should go. The app draws the cut the standard way - the tick showing which way it looks, the leader, and the gap past the walls are always the same size - and says Waiting for a cross section from <unit>. Take the section on the Tacheo (Tools → Cross section): the moment it's saved, the phone fetches it and puts it on the sketch at that cut. Cancel stops waiting.

The extended profile

The profile unrolls the passage. Each leg goes right or left by its direction compared with the first leg (more than 90° off it goes left; steeper than 80° is vertical). Change any leg's direction by tapping it on the Data page.

Close out the trip

Out of the cave, open the survey's Trip page. It starts with the close-out, step by step - each one goes green when it's done:

  1. GPS at A0 - taken, or Take it now.
  2. Shots - the legs on the phone from the Tacheo or typed. None is fine when the shots are in the book.
  3. Scan the book pages - opens the scanner (Scan sketches and data); every page goes into one PDF kept with the survey.
  4. Scan the sketches - the same, for the plan, profile and cross sections.
  5. Send it to your computer - 💻 Send it all and close out sends the survey files, the GPS point, a trip info text file (the cave, entrance, county, landowner, team and the fix) and the scans into one folder, Documents\Tacheo\From phone\day\cave - trip, then closes the survey.

A closed survey shows ✅ in the list and doesn't take shots from the Tacheo. Reopen it on the Trip page to add more. If something changes after it was sent, step 5 goes back to waiting so you know to send it again. No computer with you? Close out now, send later, and send it from the Trip page when you're back.

The Tacheo and the phone

In the Android app, connect from the Tacheo tile on Home, or from the 📡 chip at the top of a survey. On the unit: Bluetooth → Phone On. Two or more units can be connected at once - the front sight and back sight Tacheos, a SAP6 - and each shot is tagged with the unit that took it.

Each connected unit has a card, from the top:

Name, battery, signalA green dot when connected, the battery gauge and %, and the signal: four bars and a word (strong, good, fair, weak, about to drop). Under it the firmware, serial number, shots left, calibration and this trip's legs on the unit.
💬 Send a messageThe big green button. Opens Messages with this unit picked (below).
InstrumentTake a shot, Flash its laser (blinks three times - which unit is which, when two are in the same passage), Laser on, Laser off.
Which sight it takesEither, Front sight or Backsight. A backsight unit's three agreeing shots are always kept as the backsight of the leg just made; a front sight unit's never are. Each unit's shots are sorted on their own, so two people shooting at once don't mix.
Its station names follow this surveyThe phone tells this unit the station you're at after every leg (firmware 1.13 or newer), so its screen and its own trip log name the same stations. On for the first unit.
Cross sections on itThe cross sections saved on the unit (Tools → Cross section; firmware 1.16.47 or newer). Tap one to add it to the plan beside its station.
Refresh its detailsAsks the unit again for its battery and the rest.
Bluetooth: Long rangeThe unit's own Bluetooth → Long range setting, from the phone: it transmits louder (+8 dBm), and the app asks for Bluetooth's long-range radio on the phone's link where the phone has one (the card then says more power + long-range radio). It uses a little more battery. Turn long range on by itself when the signal gets weak (on by default) switches it on the moment the signal drops to one bar.
Disconnect, Switch it offDrop the link, or switch the unit off (asks first).

The signal. The phone reads each unit's signal every 2 seconds. The 📡 chip at the top shows the weakest unit's bars, and so does each unit in Messages. When a unit stays at one bar or none, the phone buzzes and says so - weak signal, stay close or about to drop - move closer - at most every 30 s (or switches it to long range, above).

Two units in a survey. On the Data page each unit has its own 🎯. In the sketch, 🎯 says which unit it fires (🎯 TCH-260004) and ⇄ beside it picks the next one.

The link comes back by itself if a unit goes out of reach for a moment (the card says reconnecting…), and the phone reconnects to the units used last when you open a survey. A shot a unit sends twice (when the phone's answer was lost) is kept once.

Messages to a unit

💬 in a survey's top bar (or 💬 Messages on the Tacheo page) opens a chat. It's one way: the phone sends, the unit shows it.

  1. Pick who gets it from the row of units at the top (a green dot = connected).
  2. Type it (up to 100 characters) and tap ➤ - or tap one of the quick messages: Shoot a backsight, LRUDs here, Re-shoot that, Set a station here, Come back to me, Good shot, Wait, Hold the laser on the station, Moving on, Done for today.
  3. It pops up on the unit over whatever page the surveyor is on, in a box in the middle with the rest of the screen dimmed, with a chime. Any button puts it away.

Under each message: ✓ sent (the unit has it), ✓✓ on screen, Read (a button was pressed), or Not read (10 min). A message that didn't go says why, with try again. Messages are kept with the trip (or on the phone, from the Tacheo page). On the unit, Settings → Messages lists the last ten with their times.

Messages need firmware 1.17.8 on a Tacheo (TD-1, T2, SAP5X, SAP5XC) or 1.16.30.5 on a SAP6. An older unit gets a note in the chat saying so.

CrossTalk live

⇄ CrossTalk on the Tacheo page watches a two-unit survey as it happens. Connect both units (the FS and the BS) to the phone; they stay linked to each other too.

  1. The top says how many units are connected to the phone and whether they're linked to each other.
  2. Each unit has a card with its signal bars, battery, role and its last shot.
  3. When one unit shoots a leg it shows straight away with Waiting for the other unit to shoot it back…. When the other one shoots, the pair shows ✓ Agree (green) or ✗ Check (red), with how far off the azimuth, clino and distance are; the one out of tolerance is in red. The phone buzzes once for agree, twice long for check.
  4. Quick messages and a message box are on the same page and go to every connected unit.
The verdict is the Tacheo's own when the unit has firmware 1.17.10 (it uses the unit's tolerances); with older firmware the phone checks the pair with the usual 2° and 0.10 ft. "Linked to each other" also needs 1.17.10.
iPhones and iPads don't let apps like this one use Bluetooth, so there shots are typed and messages can't be sent. Everything else - the sketch, the exports, sending to the computer - works the same.

Get a cave onto the phone

From your computer (easiest)

  1. Pair the phone once - see Connect to your computer.
  2. Add a cave → Caves on your computer, and pick the cave. Tacheo makes a fresh copy with its map layers and sends it over.

From a file

  1. On the computer, in CartoTable: Send to phone (File menu or the Exports page). It saves a .ctcave file to the CartoTable caves folder in your Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud or Dropbox, and asks whether to include true coordinates.
  2. On the phone: Add a cave → Open a cave file (.ctcave) and pick it from Drive, Files, a message or your downloads. On Android you can also open or share a .ctcave straight to Tacheo.

Every cave you open stays on the phone and works with no signal. Remove one from My caves.

Connect to your computer

  1. On the computer, open Tacheo and pick Connect a phone on its first screen. It shows a QR code and a 6-digit code.
  2. On the phone: My computer → Connect to my computer, then Scan the QR code (or Type the code instead). The code works for 15 minutes and pairs one phone.
  3. The phone and computer need the same Wi-Fi the first time. Tacheo has to be open on the computer (it can start with Windows - tick that in Connect a phone).

Once paired, My computer shows the caves Tacheo knows, runs Check and Reckon on the computer and shows the findings, takes files or a note from the phone, and Use its screen shows the computer's Tacheo window on the phone to use with taps and typing.

Away from home: install Tailscale on the computer and the phone and sign in to the same account on both. The app then reaches your computer from anywhere. My computer → Tailscale help walks through it. Forget this computer unpairs.

Data photos

  1. Data photos on Home. Pick the cave and the trip.
  2. Choose what you're photographing: Book pages, Sketches or Other.
  3. Take the photos with the app's camera (the light can be turned on), or choose them from the phone - several at once is fine.
  4. They're sent to your computer as soon as it can be reached, into Documents\Tacheo\From phone\day\cave - trip. Photos taken in the cave wait on the phone until there's signal, and nothing is removed from the phone until the computer has it.

On the computer, CartoTable reads the book pages into survey data (Read this page), sorts pages into trips and places the sketches. Uncertain numbers are marked for you to check against the photo.

For the best reading: the page flat and filling the frame, even light, no shadow of the phone across it, and the photo sharp.

Scan sketches and data

Like a phone's document scanner, made for survey books and sketches.

  1. Scan sketches and data on Home. Pick the cave, the trip, and what it is (Book pages, Sketches or Other).
  2. Start scanning and point the camera at the first page. The page's outline shows: orange while it's finding it, blue when it has it, green with a ring filling while you hold still.
  3. When the ring fills, the page is taken by itself: flattened square and cleaned up. The camera stays open: turn to the next page (or put the next sheet down) and hold still again. The count and the last page show at the bottom left; undo takes the last one out.
  4. The shutter button takes a page by hand at any time. Auto at the top turns taking by itself off and on; the look button switches Clean (paper white, pencil dark - the default), Colour and Black & white; 🔦 turns on the light.
  5. Done makes one PDF of all the pages, named for the cave and trip, and kept on this phone. If your computer is connected, it asks whether to send it now (or Always send by itself); it goes into Documents\Tacheo\From phone\day\cave - trip. With no computer, it waits on the phone.
Lay the page on something darker than the paper (a pack, a helmet, the table), with even light. A page bigger than the screen says "Move back"; one too small says "Move closer".

When a scan is done on the Scan page sets Send it to my computer (every time, by itself) or Ask me. Each scan on the phone has Send, Save (to the phone's Downloads) and ✕.

GPS points and your location

Take a point

  1. GPS points on Home (or 📍 Take a GPS point here on a cave's GIS tab).
  2. Tap 📍 Take a GPS point. The phone averages its readings for a few seconds; the circle goes green and the point keeps itself once it's better than ±16 ft over five readings. Keep it takes it straight away.
  3. It's named GPS 1, GPS 2 … by itself. Type a name over it first if you like, or tap the point in the list later to rename it, add a note (sink, spring, blowing hole), show it on the cave map, or delete it.

The ± is how far off the phone says it could be. Open sky helps a lot; under trees or against a bluff it can be 30 ft or more.

Send them

From the GPS points page: Send them to your computer (GPX, CSV and KML into From phone\day\GPS points), or save a GPX (GPS units and map apps), a CSV (spreadsheets and CartoTable) or a KML (Google Earth). Coordinates are WGS84, as latitude/longitude and UTM.

Your location on a cave map

On a cave that was sent with its true coordinates, tap ◎ under the N on the map, or tick Show my location on the map on the GIS tab. A blue dot shows where you are, with a circle for how sure the phone is, and the map follows you; drag the map to look around, tap ◎ to follow again, and tap it once more to switch it off. The GIS tab then shows your coordinates, the surveyed passage under you (and how far below the ground it is, where there's an elevation model), and the nearest entrance with its distance and direction. GPS points show on the map as orange diamonds. In the cave mode switches the location off to save the battery.

Looking at a cave

  • Move: drag. Zoom: pinch. Turn the plan: twist with two fingers; it turns round the station you tapped.
  • Plan / Profile / 3-D at the top switches the view. In 3-D, drag to turn it.
  • Tap a station to see how far in it is (travel time and footage), its depth, elevation, how sure its position is (the 95 % circle), its notes and lead, and the trips that surveyed it.
  • Colour the line plot by travel time from the nearest entrance, by depth, by how precise the survey is, or by trip.
  • Show the way in / out draws the quickest route from the nearest entrance as amber dashes.

With no signal

Everything about a cave on the phone works underground: the plot, stations, the way out, leads, books, trip plans and exports, and every part of Survey and sketch (Bluetooth to the Tacheo needs no signal). Map imagery shows where it was saved with Save for offline (GIS tab). Data photos wait on the phone and go to the computer later. Connecting to your computer needs Wi-Fi or Tailscale.

When something's wrong

There's no download button

The app hides it once it's installed. Inside a message app's browser, use its menu → Open in Chrome (or Safari). The downloads page always has every download.

It can't find my computer

  • Tacheo is open on the computer and Connect a phone has been set up once.
  • The phone is on the same Wi-Fi - or Tailscale is running on both.
  • Windows asked whether to allow Tacheo on networks: allow it on private networks.
  • Still not: My computer → Forget this computer, then pair again with a new code.

Android won't install it

Allow the browser to install apps when asked (Settings → Apps → your browser → Install unknown apps), and choose Install anyway if Play Protect asks.

The Tacheo isn't in the list

  • On the unit: Bluetooth → Phone On. It shows the Bluetooth sign when the phone has it.
  • The phone's Bluetooth is on, and the app was allowed Nearby devices when it asked (Settings → Apps → Tacheo → Permissions). Android 11 and older ask for location instead - Bluetooth scanning needed it then.
  • Only one phone can have the unit at a time: disconnect it in SexyTopo, TopoDroid or another phone first.

GPS doesn't work

  • Location is switched on in the phone's settings, and the app (or the browser) was allowed to use it.
  • On the Android app, location needs app 1.8. On an older app it says so; open p0sull02.github.io/cartotable-app in Chrome until the update is installed.
  • "No fix yet": go where the phone can see more sky. The first fix after the phone has been off or underground can take a minute.
  • No ◎ on the map: the cave was sent without its true coordinates. Send it again from the computer with them.

Shots come in as splays, not legs

The leg's shots didn't agree closely enough, or there were fewer than Shots per leg. Tap one of them → give it a To station, or loosen Agree within on the Trip page.

A cave doesn't open

Make a fresh copy on the computer (Send to phone, or Add a cave → Caves on your computer). Caves made by an older version of Tacheo open, but a new copy brings the newest layers.

A newer version is out

The app says so at the top of Home. On Android, download it from the downloads page and install over the old one - your caves stay.
